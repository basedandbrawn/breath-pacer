Second Wind

The app is called Breath today. These four directions assume it becomes Second Wind, the breath you catch between sets, so the name says what the app is for and the word WIND can carry the whole identity. If you keep a different name, every treatment below works on any name that contains the word.

Each direction has a home screen and a live session screen. The session screens run a real 4 · 2 · 6 cycle. Pin all of them on one phase to compare, and switch on the guides to see the fixed slots that keep the layout from moving.

The wind

On the home screen a laminar field flows left to right and parts around SECOND WIND, the way smoke parts around a wing in a wind tunnel. In a session the field turns radial: air pours into the center on the inhale, hangs suspended on the hold, and streams outward on the exhale. A soft halo at the center brightens as the lungs fill.

The wind

On the home screen the sock flies in a gusting crosswind above the name, with a live wind report in knots. In a session the sock is the instrument: it fills band by band on the inhale, stands rigid on the hold, and drops on the exhale. The convention is real: a sock stands fully out at 15 knots, about 3 knots per band, so five bands and a full breath reads 15 KT.

  • Save set and Skip rest share one full-width bar. The slot is reserved even when empty.
  • Hold sets: the sock stays inflated and the bands count down seconds elapsed.

The wind

Anybody has a width axis from 50 to 150. On the home screen the four letters ripple one after another, a flag in a light gust. In a session the phase word and count sit still at the top; below them the name is the gauge, widening as you breathe in, holding wide, narrowing as you let go. The word grows from its own centre, so nothing around it shifts.

The wind

On the home screen the anemometer idles and gusts inside a compass ring; the name sits below it like an engraved nameplate. In a session the needle climbs to 12 on the inhale, holds at 12 while the cups stop, and falls back on the exhale. The cups spin fastest mid-inhale, when the air moves most.

Why nothing moves between IN, HOLD and OUT

Every direction uses the same slot stack. Only colour and the instrument animate; every box keeps its height and position for the whole session.

  1. One font size for the phase word. Today fitWord() in index.html refits the size for each word, so IN renders larger than HOLD and the ring contents jump. Fit once for the longest word (HOLD, or GET SET), then leave the size alone.
  2. Fixed-height slots. Word, count, rail and action each have a set height. Content is centred inside, so a two-letter word and a four-letter word share the same baseline.
  3. Tabular numerals with reserved width. The count uses font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums and a two-character minimum, so 10 and 4 occupy the same space.
  4. All three phase labels always render. The rail shows IN · HOLD · OUT the whole time and only changes emphasis. No label appears or disappears.
  5. Hidden controls keep their slot. Save set, Skip rest and Back share one reserved footer. Use visibility: hidden, never display: none, so the footer height is constant.
  6. Colour crossfades, geometry does not. Background and text colour ease over 500 ms per phase. The instrument lives inside a fixed box; the halo, sock, letters or needle move within it.
