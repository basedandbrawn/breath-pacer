Eight winds, seven tones and five rep shapes for a breath pacer that lives in a gym. Every wind on this page follows the one breath clock below, so you can compare them mid-breath. Every tone plays from your speaker on a tap. The rep shapes run a real three-rep set with the cues you would hear at the bar.

01 · Wind

Eight winds

The test for each one is peripheral: cover the caption and stand six feet away. You should still tell in from hold from out. Each wind is a different mechanism, not a different skin, and each says what a hold looks like, because the hold is where most wind animations fail. All run on a 2D canvas at a fraction of the app's GPU cost.

02 · Tone

Seven tones, one note each

None of these change pitch and none strike a second note. The breath is carried by how the one note opens and closes: brighter and fuller as you breathe in, darker and softer as you breathe out. That is the shape of the best 4·7·8 and 5.5 coherence tracks, and it is the opposite of an alarm: no transient, no interval, nothing above two kilohertz, and nothing that could not be predicted a second ahead. Tap Play to hear two cycles of the pattern chosen above.

03 · Lift

Five rep shapes

The gym failure was a breath pacer showing breath words. Under a load you need the movement word, with the breath carried underneath it: as colour, as the tone, as a second track. So every mode here names what the body does, and the breath bar runs across movement segments when the same breath continues. In Squeeze the exhale is one bar under PULL and SQUEEZE, because it is one exhale.

04 · Notes

The rear delt fly, answered

Do I stop breathing while squeezing, or keep breathing out?

Keep breathing out. The squeeze is the tail of the same exhale that drove the pull. Air leaving through the squeeze is what keeps the ribs down and the contraction honest. If you run out of air before the squeeze ends, sit empty for the last moment; do not inhale until you start lowering.

What is my breathing doing during the eccentric?

Breathing in. The lowering is where the air comes back, all of it, unhurried, for the whole length of the lowering. The old Squeeze mode never said so: it opened every cycle on IN with no movement word, so IN had to be guessed as the lowering.

Longer in, longer out, or the same?

Neither is the goal in the set. The breath follows the movement tempo: the exhale is as long as pull plus squeeze, the inhale as long as the lowering. For a machine rear delt fly that is about 3 out and 3 in. A long exhale (4·7·8, 4·6) is for coming down between sets and after, not for the rep.

Why did OUT then SQUEEZE feel like two things?

Because the app scheduled them as two phases with two words, and the tone stopped on the squeeze. Two words for one breath is a sequence to follow; one bar under two movements is a shape to feel. The tone must carry through the squeeze as the same note, still closing.
