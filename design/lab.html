The app, five ways

This is the engine that now ships in wind.html, running live under each palette. Slate is the default: slate ground, muted blue air that warms to sand on the way out. Parchment is paper, with the air as ink. Every palette recolours the type too, so Parchment is dark ink on a light page rather than white text on cream. Tap one to open it big.

02 · Wind

Twelve winds

The test for each one is peripheral: cover the caption and stand six feet away. You should still tell in from hold from out. Each is a different mechanism, not a different skin, and each says what a hold looks like, because the hold is where wind animations fail. The four new ones are at the end.

03 · Tone

Two notes, never confused

The reference tracks agree on one thing. Coherence's 2 Bells cues the in with a high bell and the out with a low bell; the Harmonium track rises for the in and falls for the out; Sym uses two bowl tones. So the breath in is one note and the breath out is another, a fifth below, and you always know which you are on. Nothing slides between them and nothing climbs a scale: each note opens and closes in place. Tap Play for the pattern chosen above.

04 · Lift

Three words

Brace, drive, set, squeeze, top: five words to learn under a load was the problem, and SET was the worst of them. Now there are three, and they are the ones every gym already uses. LOWER is the way down. LIFT is the way up. HOLD is any pause. The breath is the line under the word, and it is the only thing that changes from lift to lift: whether a HOLD keeps the air in or keeps letting it out. The set opens on READY, which is where you breathe in.

05 · Notes

The questions

What was SET supposed to mean?

The one breath in you take after you are in position and before the first pull: handles in hand, chest up, breathe in, then go. It is a real thing lifters do, and it was a bad word for it, because SET already means a set of reps and it named a breath rather than a movement. It is gone. READY is that breath now: the count-in says "breathe in" under it, and the first movement word follows.

Why were brace, drive, squeeze and top confusing?

Each was a coaching cue for one kind of lift, so there were five words for two things the body does. Under a load you cannot hold a vocabulary. The way down and the way up are the same for every lift, so those are the words: LOWER and LIFT. A pause is a pause: HOLD. What differs between lifts is only what the air does in the hold, and that is the small line under the word, not a new word.

Why was the wind slow?

Layers. A GPU fluid with a depth pass, a bloom, dye that faded in a second, a frame clamp that played slow motion when the phone fell behind, and a force scaled by how loud the tone happened to be. The rebuild is one canvas: lines with momentum, a breath that pulls them in and sends them out, and nothing else between the breath and the screen. It runs at 60 fps at a third of a millisecond.

Why two notes and not one?

One note that opens and closes can be read either way if you tune in halfway. Two notes cannot: the high one is always the in, the low one always the out. The reference tracks all do this, with bells, bowls or a harmonium. Nothing about it is a melody: each note stays on its pitch and only its brightness and level move.

Reference tracks: Coherence 2 Bells (high bell in, low bell out), Coherent Breathing audio (Long Soft Bell, Harmonium Breathing, Six Bridges), Sym Breathing (bowl tones), Coherent Breathing Timer Bells 5.5.
